The collection

Some cars are bought. These were chosen. A Dearborn original, an Ohio-built hybrid and a grand tourer from Maranello: three answers to the same question, kept under one roof on Atlanta's Westside.

I

1965 Ford Mustang

Dearborn, Michigan · United States

Chapter one

The one that started the stampede.

Unveiled at the New York World's Fair on April 17, 1964, it invented a whole category overnight. People still call it the pony car.

289cu in

Small-block V8

In K-code "Hi-Po" trim the 289 made 271 horsepower. It revs high, sounds glorious and has no electronics to hide behind.

Proportion

Long hood. Short deck.

Every pony car since has borrowed that silhouette. It still turns heads at a light on Peachtree.

II

2019 Honda NSX

Marysville, Ohio · Sold here as the Acura NSX

Chapter two

A supercar that learned to think.

A twin-turbo V6 and three electric motors, hand-assembled at the Performance Manufacturing Center in Marysville, Ohio.

573hp combined

3.5 L twin-turbo V6, mid-mounted

One motor sits between the engine and gearbox. Two more drive the front wheels on their own, pulling the nose into corners.

9speed dual-clutch

Sport Hybrid SH-AWD

Gears change in milliseconds and torque-vectoring works quietly underneath. It feels less like a hybrid and more like a very fast conversation.

III

2020 Ferrari Roma

Maranello · Italy

Chapter three

Unhurried. Devastating.

Unveiled in Rome in November 2019, it chose a long, clean line over a spoiler. It is the quiet one in the room.

620cv

3.9 L twin-turbo V8, front-mid

The V8 sits behind the front axle and sends 612 horsepower to the rear wheels through an eight-speed dual-clutch.

3.4sec · 0–100 km/h

Beyond 320 km/h

Almost none of that speed is needed on I-85. That is rather the point.
